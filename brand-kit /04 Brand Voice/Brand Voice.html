FOLDER 04 · BRAND VOICE

The gentleman's register.

Stitch Fit speaks the way a senior tailor does — calm, sure of itself, never raising its voice. Words are chosen with the same care given to a seam. We sell with composure, not volume.

FOUR PILLARS · EVERY SENTENCE MUST HOLD ONE
i.
Considered
We do not rush a sentence the way we do not rush a stitch. Read every line aloud. Remove the word it does not need.
ii.
Confident
Declarative, never hedged. We say "this fits" — not "we hope this might suit you." Stand behind the work.
iii.
Warm
Authority without coldness. A first name in greeting. Plain language for the business at hand. A small joke when the moment allows.
iv.
Crafted
Choose the older word over the new one. Use the language of the trade — yarn, weave, gauge, seam — without parading it.

SIX RULES OF THE PEN
Lead with the noun.
"Linen. Long-staple, hand-finished. Cut for the warmer months." — not "Our team has crafted this exceptional linen."
Cut every fourth word.
Test the sentence after one round of cuts. If meaning survives, keep cutting.
Italics for emphasis, never bold.
A whispered this one outperforms a shouted THIS ONE.
Names of cloth get reverence.
Merino. Cashmere. Pima. Always capitalise the noble fibres. They earned it.
Numbers in long form for ceremony.
"Cut from the same loom since nineteen twenty-eight." Numerals for spec sheets only.
No exclamation marks. Ever.
The brand never shouts. If a sentence needs an exclamation to land, it was not the right sentence.
